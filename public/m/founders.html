What an Illinois LLC is and why founders choose one

A limited liability company (LLC) is a business structure that separates the business from its owners. When you form an Illinois LLC and keep your business finances separate, your personal assets are generally shielded from the business's debts. That separation is a big reason many solo founders and small teams start with an LLC.

LLCs also keep taxes simple. By default, a single-member LLC's profit flows through to the owner's personal return, and a multi-member LLC is taxed as a partnership. An LLC can also elect corporate tax treatment, and a CPA can tell you which option fits your situation.

1

Step 1: Choose your LLC name

Your name must include "Limited Liability Company," "LLC," or "L.L.C." and must be distinguishable from businesses already registered in Illinois. Search the business database on the Illinois Secretary of State website before you commit. If you are not ready to file, you can reserve a name for 90 days for a small fee.

Check whether a matching domain name is available at the same time. It makes building your website much easier later.

2

Step 2: Name an Illinois registered agent

Illinois requires every LLC to have a registered agent: a person or company with an Illinois street address who accepts lawsuits, legal notices, and state mail for the LLC during normal business hours.

Why not use your home? The registered agent's address is public record. If you serve as your own agent from home, your home address appears in the state's business database. You can act as your own agent, hire a registered agent service, or choose Founders, which includes registered agent service for your first year.

3

Step 3: Get a business address

Your LLC also needs a principal office address. It is where business mail goes, what your website shows, and what appears on invoices. A physical business mailbox gives you a real street address instead of a PO Box, with 24/7 mailbox access at Workspace Junction.

4

Step 4: File your Articles of Organization

The Articles of Organization create your LLC. You file them with the Illinois Secretary of State online or by mail. The form asks for your LLC name, your principal office address, your registered agent's name and Illinois address, the names and addresses of your organizers and managers or members, and your business purpose, which can be general.

Illinois LLC filing fee. Approx. $150 Illinois state filing fee paid separately to Illinois. This fee may change and is not included in the package price. Confirm the current amount with the Illinois Secretary of State before you file. Processing times vary, and no filing service can guarantee approval.

5

Step 5: Get an EIN and open a bank account

An Employer Identification Number (EIN) is your LLC's federal tax ID. You need one if your LLC has employees or more than one member, and most banks ask for one. Apply for an EIN free at IRS.gov. Then open a business bank account. Keeping business and personal money separate supports your liability protection and makes bookkeeping easier.

6

Step 6: Build your website

A 5-page website is enough to start: home, about, services, contact, and a privacy policy. It gives customers a place to find you, confirm you are a real business, and reach out. Founders includes a 5-page custom website with hosting, management, and two rounds of revisions.

After formation, Illinois LLCs file an annual report with the Secretary of State to stay in good standing. Put the due date on your calendar.

How long does it take to form an LLC in Illinois?

Processing times depend on how you file and on the Illinois Secretary of State's current workload. Online filings are usually faster than mail, and expedited service may be available for an extra fee. Check the Secretary of State website for current timelines.

Do I need a registered agent in Illinois?

Yes. Every Illinois LLC must name a registered agent with an Illinois street address who is available during normal business hours to accept legal notices for the LLC. The Founders Package includes registered agent service for one year.

Can I use my home address for my LLC?

Yes, but addresses on your filing become public record. Many owners use a commercial business address, like the mailbox included in Founders, to keep their home address private.

Do I need an EIN for my Illinois LLC?

You need an EIN if your LLC has more than one member or has employees, and most banks ask for one to open a business account. Applying is free at IRS.gov.
